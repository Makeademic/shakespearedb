Provenance notes From bookseller's catalogue: "Later endpaper cut-out on front pastedown to reveal ownership inscription of George Frere (1774-1854), earlier ownership inscription “E. R. Grant” (Elizabeth Raper Grant), his future wife, to an early blank .... The ownership inscription “M Raper” at the head of the title page of Othello, a smaller, neater hand than the cast list, must be that of one of the silk-trading brothers Moses or Matthew Raper (1680-1748), both directors of the Bank of England, or of the latter’s son, the antiquarian Matthew Raper the younger (1705-1778). On the latter’s death, his manor house at Thorley, Hertfordshire, passed to his brother John, whose granddaughter was Elizabeth Raper Grant (1778-1850), who in 1806 married George Frere (1774-1854); his ownership inscription is retained beneath the cut-out paper on the front pastedown. Matthew Raper the younger, a friend of Benjamin Franklin, was elected to the Royal Society on 30 May 1754 and was awarded the Copley Medal in 1771 for a paper 'Inquiry into the Value of the ancient Greek and Roman Money.'”

Marginalia From bookseller's catalogue: "annotated with textual comparisons with the first quarto of 1622, and prefaced by a unique manuscript cast list from c.1691-2 naming actors involved in a notorious murder trial with dramatic echoes of Shakespeare’s tragedy .... The textual annotations are interesting, in that they compare the 1655 readings with those of the first quarto of 1622, rather than the folio text published the following year .... The unpublished manuscript cast list written on the blank facing the title is a remarkable survival, written shortly before two key names mentioned in it became embroiled in one of the most lurid murder trials of the age, with startling echoes of Othello. The manuscript evidently names the cast for a revival of the play by the United Company, almost certainly at Drury Lane. The title role is played by the legendary actor-manager Thomas Betterton (1635-1710) .... The cast list here has “Mis Bracegirdle” as Desdemona, “Mis Knight” as Emilia, and “Mis Richerson” as Bianco [sic]. Anne Bracegirdle (1671-1748) had been in the foster care of Betterton and his wife since infancy. Her first recorded performance was 12 January 1688 for the United Company, and she graduated to leading roles around 1689-90. “Mis Knight” is Frances Maria Knight. “Mis Richerson” is Mrs Richardson, whose best attested dates of activity are 1690-92. Playing Roderigo is Mr Alexander, the stage name of John Verbruggen; in the role of Cassio is William Mountfort. The cast list must therefore date between October 1690, when Verbruggen joined the company, and 10 December 1692, when William Mountfort was murdered. Around this time, Anne Bracegirdle was being stalked by an unwanted admirer, Captain Richard Hill. He was jealous of the dashing Mountfort and imagined that Mountfort had captured the actress’s heart. On 9 December 1692 Hill and a companion, Charles, Lord Mohun, attempted to abduct Bracegirdle on her way back from a dinner party. Mountfort came by on his way home and Hill stabbed him. Hill fled to the Isle of Wight and Mohun stood trial before the House of Lords as his accomplice. Frances Maria Knight was among those who gave evidence. Lord Mohun’s acquittal caused a scandal. A journalist was taken into custody for saying the evidence was strong enough to hang a commoner. After Mountfort’s death, his widow married John Verbruggen."
