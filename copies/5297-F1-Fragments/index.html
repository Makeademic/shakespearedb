Copy Details for Comedies, Histories, and Tragedies: F1 (1623)

University of California,Los Angeles,Clark Library

From Bloomsbury catalogue: "A1 ('To the Reader'), A2 (title with portrait) and A5 (commendatory verses) plus 8 ff. of main text (c2 and last 7 ff.) all replaced in modern facsimile, 7 further preliminary ff. supplied from another, slightly shorter copy, A1 (first f. of The Tempest) mounted & silked, 4 other ff. silked, a few ff. with later repair to fore-corners, some minor marking and browning, with very occasional 18th century marginalia ... 6 later ms. ff. bound in supplying additional section of Cymbeline ... The facsimile leaves are supplied from the 1909 Methuen edition, sympathetically toned to fit." From Sotheby's catalogue: "Tipped-in at the beginning is a typed note recording that this copy was rebound by Edward Lambert in the Bookbinding Department of Leeds Public Libraries in April 1959 ("...The previous binding was in brown half leather...some crude repair work on a few of the first and last leaves was removed and new repair using silk lisse substituted..."). Most of the missing leaves from this copy are supplied in photofacsimile from the copy in the Sterling Library in the University of London Library, bound in brown cloth in a separate volume included with this lot."

Provenance notes Sold at Bloomsbury Auctions, 15 May 2012, £24,400, buyer unknown. This appears to be the same copy that went unsold at Sotheby's, 7 Dec 2006; provenance and other details supplied from Sotheby's catalogue. From Sotheby's catalogue: "this second folio has been owned by the distinguished Dawson family of Langcliffe Hall, Yorkshire since the seventeenth century. A very early, if not its first, owner was Josias Dawson of Halton Gill, who married Mary Foster of Langcliffe Hall in 1642 (Josias's elder son Christopher was at Cambridge with Isaac Newton, who is said to have visited him on his estate later in life). Josias's great-grandson Pudsey Dawson (1752-1816) was mayor of Liverpool in 1779 and 1780. Josias's great-great-grandson Pudsey Dawson II (1778-1859) was a founding Director of the North West Railway. He sold Langcliffe Hall in the early 1830s to the Swale family of Settle, but the estate was re-acquired in 1860 by Miss Elizabeth Hutton Dawson of Marshfield, Settle. Geoffrey Dawson (nee Robinson, b.1874), who inherited the estate in 1917, was the editor of The Times from 1910 to 1942 (with a short break between 1919 and 1922)." From Sotheby's catalogue: "pencil inscriptions on front endpaper recording the provenance of the Dawson family ("Josias Dawson Esq. | Halton Gill | ...Dawsons | Rectory Bolton... | M.L. & [?] G.H. Dawson | late of the Rectory Bolton | Marshfield Sept 1826")"
