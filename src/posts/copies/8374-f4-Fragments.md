  Note by Malone on endpaper: "This edition of some of Shak-speare’s Poems is so very rare, that I have never seen another copy; nor is it mentioned in any catalogue ancient or modern.\r\n All the poems from Signature D5 were written by Thomas Heywood, who was so offended at Jaggard for printing them under the name of Shakespeare that he has added a postscript to his Apology for Actors, 4to 1612, on this subject; and Jaggard in consequence of it appears to have printed a new title-page to please Heywood, without the name of Shakespeare in it. Heywood thus speaks of this in the postscript above-mentioned: 'Here likewise I must necessarily insert a manifest injury done to me in that worke [Britayne’s Troy] by taking the two epistles of Paris to Helen and Helen to Paris, and printing them in a less volume under the name of another, wch may put the world in opinion I might steale them from him, and hee, to do himselfe right, hath since published them in his owne name; but as I must acknowledge my lines not worthy his patro-nage under whom he hath publisht them, or the author, I know, much offended with Mr Jaggard, that (altogether unknown to him) presumed to make so bold with his name.'\r\nIn consequence of Jaggard’s conduct, these translations from Ovid for near a century and a half passed for Shakespeare’s, when at length my friend Dr Farmer, in 1766, pointed out the occasion of the mistake.\r\n                                    E.M.\r\n                                    Octr 22, 1785"
